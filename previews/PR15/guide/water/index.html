Water chemistry

Liquors carry their dissolved Minerals: calcium, magnesium, sodium, chloride, sulfate and alkalinity (as CaCO₃, negative once acids took more than it), conserved like everything else. They mix, drain with the liquor, and concentrate in the boil.

Water profiles

A water is given by its WaterProfile, the concentrations of its ions, as in a water report:

tap = WaterProfile(calcium = 30u"mg/L", magnesium = 8u"mg/L", sodium = 12u"mg/L",
                   chloride = 25u"mg/L", sulfate = 30u"mg/L", bicarbonate = 120u"mg/L")
w = water(16u"L"; temperature = 75u"°C", profile = tap)
w.minerals
Minerals(480.0 mg, 128.0 mg, 192.0 mg, 400.0 mg, 480.0 mg, 1574.7108955028189 mg, 1920.0 mg)

profile gives the profile of any liquor or contents, and residual_alkalinity Kolbach's residual alkalinity:

(profile(w), residual_alkalinity(w))
(WaterProfile(Ca 30.0, Mg 8.0, Na 12.0, Cl 25.0, SO₄ 30.0, HCO₃ 120.0 mg/L), 72.30797908389134 mg L^-1)

Salts and acids

Salts and Acids are lots that dissolve where they are added: their ions go to the liquor, and an acid takes alkalinity. The salts and acids are those of BrewingFormulas (Gypsum, CalciumChloride, EpsomSalt, MagnesiumChloride, TableSalt, BakingSoda, Chalk, SlakedLime, LacticAcid, PhosphoricAcid).

q = single_batch()
q[:strike_water] = water(16u"L"; temperature = 75u"°C", profile = tap)
add!(q, :gypsum, Salt(Gypsum()) => 4u"g")
add!(q, :lactic, Acid(LacticAcid()) => 1u"mL")
connect!(q, [:gypsum, :lactic] => :mash)
res = brew(q)
profile(res[:wort])
WaterProfile(Ca 56.4, Mg 5.1, Na 7.7, Cl 16.0, SO₄ 108.3, HCO₃ 48.7 mg/L)

Mash pH

mash_ph estimates the pH of the mash of a node before its mash rest, by the balance of charges of BrewingFormulas' mash_ph: the distilled water pH and buffering of each grain, and the residual alkalinity and volume of the water.

mash_ph(res[:mash])
5.62892077172718

Pale base malt is taken with a distilled water pH of 5.7 and a buffering of 40 mEq/(kg·pH). Crystal and roasted malts lower the pH much more, so they need their own distilled water pH (Fermentable(...; di_ph = 4.7), with an optional buffering); a grain darker than 8 SRM without one is taken as base malt, with a warning.

From BeerJSON

The importer reads the water additions of a recipe as the profile of its water, its "water agent" additions as salts and acids, and the distilled water pH of fermentable records.
