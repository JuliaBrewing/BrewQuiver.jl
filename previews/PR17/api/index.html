API reference

BrewQuiver.BrewQuiver — Module
BrewQuiver

The engine of the BrewQuiver brewing software: the brewing process as a quiver, a directed acyclic multigraph whose nodes are the Contents of vessels at the stages of the brewing (the lots of ingredients, the water, the mash, the wort in the kettle, the beer in the fermentor, ...) and whose arrows are the processes between them: transfers, drains and transformations (mash rests, boils, fermentations, ...). Streams can be split and merged, as in parti-gyle brewing or blending.

The state of each node holds conserved quantities (a Liquor and solids), so that mixing and splitting are exact; gravity, alcohol, bitterness and color are computed with BrewingFormulas.jl.

using Unitful, UnitfulBrewing, BrewingFormulas, BrewQuiver

pale = Fermentable("Pale ale malt"; yield = 80u"percent", color = 3u"°L")
q = Quiver()
add!(q, :pale, pale => 5u"kg")
add!(q, :strike_water, water(16u"L"; temperature = 75u"°C"))
add!(q, :mash, :rested; vessel = :mashtun)
connect!(q, [:strike_water, :pale] => :mash)
connect!(q, :mash => :rested, MashRest(temperature = 67u"°C"))
...
res = brew(q)

Liquors

BrewQuiver.Liquor — Type
Liquor(; water = 0u"kg", extract = 0u"kg", alcohol = 0u"kg", isoalpha = 0u"mg",
         color = 0.0, co2 = 0u"g", temperature = 20u"°C", minerals = Minerals())

The state of a liquid (water, wort, beer, ...), given by its conserved quantities:

  • water: mass of water (a volume is taken at 20 °C);
  • extract: mass of dissolved (real) extract;
  • alcohol: mass of ethanol;
  • isoalpha: mass of iso-alpha acids;
  • color: color units, as lb × °L (malt color units times volume in gal);
  • co2: mass of dissolved CO₂;
  • temperature;
  • minerals: the Minerals dissolved.

Liquors are mixed with + and split with * by a fraction. Derived quantities: volume, gravity, original_gravity, real_extract, abw, abv, ibu, srm, carbonation. See also water and wort.

Water chemistry

BrewQuiver.Minerals — Type
Minerals(; calcium = 0u"mg", magnesium = 0u"mg", sodium = 0u"mg", chloride = 0u"mg",
           sulfate = 0u"mg", alkalinity = 0u"mg", other = 0u"mg")

The minerals dissolved in a liquor, as conserved quantities: the masses of calcium, magnesium, sodium, chloride and sulfate, the alkalinity as a mass of CaCO₃ (the equivalents of bicarbonate, carbonate and hydroxide, less those of the acids added; it can be negative), and the mass of the other species dissolved (bicarbonate, carbonate, hydroxide, acids, water of crystallization), so that the mass of the salts and acids added is conserved. They are mixed with + and split with * by a fraction, with the liquor. See WaterProfile.

source

               chloride = 0u"mg/L", sulfate = 0u"mg/L", bicarbonate = 0u"mg/L")

The concentrations of the main ions of a water (in mg/L or ppm), e.g. a water report or a classic brewing water. See water and profile.

source
BrewQuiver.profile — Function

source
BrewingFormulas.residual_alkalinity — Method
residual_alkalinity(liquor), residual_alkalinity(contents)

source
BrewingFormulas.mash_ph — Method
mash_ph(contents)

An estimate of the pH of a mash with the contents of a node before its mash rest (the grain and the water, with the salts and acids added to it), by the balance of charges of BrewingFormulas' mash_ph: the distilled water pH and buffering of each grain (see Fermentable; pale base malt by default), and the residual alkalinity and volume of the liquor. Grains darker than 8 SRM without a distilled water pH are taken as base malt, with a warning, since crystal and roasted malts lower the pH much more.

res = brew(q)
mash_ph(res[:mash])

BrewQuiver.Fermentable — Type
Fermentable(name; kind = :grain, yield = 80u"percent", color = 2u"SRM", moisture = 4u"percent",
            di_ph = nothing, buffering = nothing)
Fermentable(name; potential = 1.037u"sg", ...)

source
BrewQuiver.Salt — Type
Salt(salt::AbstractSalt; name)

A brewing salt (an AbstractSalt of BrewingFormulas: Gypsum(), CalciumChloride(), EpsomSalt(), ...). A lot of it, Salt(Gypsum()) => 5u"g", holds its ions dissolved (see Minerals), so that they mix with the water it is added to.

source
BrewQuiver.Acid — Type
Acid(acid::AbstractAcid; name)

An acid solution (an AbstractAcid of BrewingFormulas: LacticAcid(), PhosphoricAcid()). A lot of it, Acid(LacticAcid()) => 2u"mL" (or a mass), holds its acid dissolved, lowering the alkalinity of the water it is added to by its milliequivalents at pH 5.4.

source
BrewQuiver.HOP_FORM_FACTORS — Constant
HOP_FORM_FACTORS

Utilization factors of the hop forms, relative to leaf hops: pellets and powders are taken to give 10% more bitterness. It can be modified to change the defaults.

The 10% for pellets is a homebrewing convention, not part of Tinseth's or Rager's models. The Python BrewQuiver divided Tinseth's utilization by 0.9 and used the factors 1 for pellets and 0.9 for leaf hops, i.e. pellets about 11% above leaf hops.

AbstractArrow

Supertype of the arrows of a Quiver: Transfer, Drain and the transformations (AbstractTransformation).

source
BrewQuiver.AbstractTransformation — Type
AbstractTransformation <: AbstractArrow

Supertype of the arrows that turn all the contents of a node into the next state of the same vessel: MashRest, Boil, Chill, Ferment, Prime, Carbonate. A transformation must be the only arrow leaving its node. A new one implements transform.

source
BrewQuiver.transform — Function
transform(t::AbstractTransformation, contents::Contents, models) -> (contents, released)

The contents after the transformation t, and what it released from the quiver, as a NamedTuple (water, co2) of masses (negative for what it took in).

source
BrewQuiver.Transfer — Type
Transfer(amount = nothing; remaining = nothing, at = nothing, duration = 0u"minute")

Moves a uniform part of the contents of a node: a volume (of the liquor), a mass, a fraction (a percentage or a plain number from 0 to 1), or nothing for what is left by the other arrows (shared equally among the transfers of the rest).

The ingredients moved arrive with the timing remaining or at (see Remaining, At), e.g. Transfer(20u"g"; remaining = 10u"minute") for a hop with 10 minutes of boil left. The transfer takes the time duration (see timeline).

source
BrewQuiver.Drain — Type
Drain(; absorption = nothing, retain = nothing, duration = nothing, hop_absorption = nothing)

Moves the liquid of a node, leaving behind its solids, the liquid absorbed by the grain (absorption per mass of grain, see grist), the liquid absorbed by the hops (hop_absorption per mass of hops, fresh or spent, e.g. after a dry hop) and the volume retain (dead space, trub, yeast cake). It acts on what the transfers of amounts leave, before the transfers of the rest; a node has at most one drain. Draining takes the time duration (see timeline).

What is not given comes from the Vessel of the node, if it is equipped (see equip!): its grain absorption, its dead space, and its drain rate for the duration; or else 1.0 L/kg of grain absorption, no hop absorption, nothing retained and no time.

source
BrewQuiver.MashRest — Type
MashRest(; temperature = 67u"°C", time = 60u"minute", conversion = 95u"percent")

A mash rest: the grain in the node gives the fraction conversion of its potential extract (and its moisture and color) to the liquor, at temperature, leaving its Residue, the spent grain. Soluble fermentables dissolve completely.

source
BrewQuiver.Boil — Type
Boil(; time = 60u"minute", evaporation = nothing, stand = 0u"minute",
       stand_temperature = 80u"°C")

Boils the contents of a node for time, evaporating water at the rate evaporation (a volume, or a fraction of the volume, per time; if not given, the evaporation rate of the Vessel of the node, or else 4 L/h) and driving off the CO₂, followed by a hop stand (or whirlpool) of stand at stand_temperature. Soluble fermentables dissolve; each hop isomerizes during its own time in the boil (see AbstractTiming) and in the stand, with the utilization model models.bitterness at the mean of the gravities before and after the boil and the factor HOP_FORM_FACTORS of its form, leaving its HopMatter. A time in the stand counts as isomerization_factor(stand_temperature) times as much boil, by the isomerization rate of BrewingFormulas.

The timing of the hops counts from the boil: Remaining is the time left in the boil, before the stand, and At a time from the start of the boil, so that a hop added At(time + 10u"minute") stands for the last stand - 10u"minute". The boil and the stand take time + stand (see duration), and the wort is left at stand_temperature after a stand.

source
BrewQuiver.Chill — Type
Chill(; temperature = 20u"°C", time = 0u"minute")

Brings the contents of a node to temperature, e.g. the wort after the boil, before it is transferred to the fermentor. Nothing else changes (the volumes of the quiver are at 20 °C).

source
BrewQuiver.Ferment — Type
Ferment(; temperature = 20u"°C", time = 14u"d", attenuation = nothing)

Ferments the contents of a node with the cultures in it, down to the apparent attenuation of its original extract: by default, the largest attenuation of the cultures present. Soluble fermentables dissolve first.

By Balling, each 2.0665 g of extract give 1 g of ethanol, 0.9565 g of CO₂ and 0.11 g of yeast, which joins the YeastCake. The CO₂ escapes except for the residual CO₂ at temperature (see residual_co2).

source
BrewQuiver.Prime — Type
Prime(; sugar = CornSugar(), time = 14u"d")

Bottle or keg conditioning: the soluble fermentables in the node (the priming sugar) are fully fermented, and their CO₂ stays dissolved. The CO₂ yield is that of sugar, an AbstractPrimingSugar of BrewingFormulas; equal numbers of moles of ethanol and CO₂ are produced, and the water balances the masses (the water of a monohydrate is released, the water of the hydrolysis of sucrose is taken). See priming_mass for the mass of sugar giving a carbonation level. The conditioning takes the given time.

source
BrewQuiver.Carbonate — Type
Carbonate(carbonation; time = 2u"d")

Forced carbonation of the contents of a node to the carbonation level, with CO₂ from outside the quiver (released as a negative mass), taking the given time.

source
BrewQuiver.priming_mass — Function
priming_mass(contents, carbonation; sugar = CornSugar(), eq = SugarGravityQuad())

The mass of priming sugar that Prime needs to bring contents to the carbonation level, accounting for the CO₂ already dissolved and for the change of volume due to the ethanol and water produced.

Quivers

BrewQuiver.Quiver — Type
Quiver()

An empty quiver: the brewing process as a directed acyclic graph whose nodes, labelled by symbols, are the Contents of vessels at the stages of the brewing, and whose arrows (AbstractArrow) are the processes between them: transfers, drains and transformations. Several arrows may join the same two nodes.

Build it with add! and connect!, and compute it with brew.

source
BrewQuiver.Node — Type
Node

A node of a Quiver: its initial contents (the lots on the shelf, the water; empty for the stages filled by arrows), the vessel it is in, if given, and view, free data for interfaces (a position, a color, ...; see nodeview), kept by the files of quivers.

source

Adds to q a node with initial contents (a lot: a liquor such as water(16u"L"), or ingredient => amount), or empty nodes, to be filled by arrows, with the given labels. They are in vessel, if given.

BrewQuiver.connect! — Function
connect!(q::Quiver, source => destination, arrow = Transfer())

Adds an arrow (a Transfer, Drain or transformation) from the node source to the node destination, e.g.

Brewing

BrewQuiver.brew — Function
brew(q::Quiver; models = (;))

Computes all the nodes and arrows of q, in topological order, with the models of BrewingFormulas in models (a NamedTuple overriding BrewingFormulas' DEFAULT_MODELS, e.g. (bitterness = Rager(), color = Daniels())). Returns a BrewResult.

source
BrewQuiver.rebrew! — Function
rebrew!(res::BrewResult, q::Quiver, label)

Updates res after a change of the node label of q (its initial contents) or of the arrows leaving it, recomputing only label and the nodes downstream of it.

Other changes of the structure of q since res was computed are detected and recomputed too: the nodes added, the sources and destinations of the arrows removed (by disconnect! or remove!), and the sources of new arrows. Changes of existing arrows are not detected, so give the label of their source.

Timeline

BrewQuiver.duration — Function
duration(arrow::AbstractArrow)

How long the arrow takes: the duration of a transfer or a drain, the time of a transformation. A new arrow type with a duration implements this method; the default is no time. See timeline.

source
BrewQuiver.timeline — Function
timeline(q::Quiver; start = 0u"minute", models = (;)) -> Timeline
timeline(res::BrewResult, q::Quiver; start = 0u"minute") -> Timeline

The timeline of q: when the contents of each node arrive, are all in, and leave, and when each arrow runs, from the durations of the arrows, as early as the arrows allow (the longest paths through the quiver).

  • The lots (nodes without predecessors) are ready at start.
  • A node is ready when all the streams reaching it (the arrows from other nodes) have arrived. The lots transferred to it without a timing arrive then, e.g. the yeast pitched when the wort is in the fermentor; with a timing (Remaining, At), they arrive during the transformation that follows, e.g. a hop with 10 minutes of boil left.
  • A transformation starts when its node is ready; the transfers of amounts and the drain too, and the transfers of the rest when those end, e.g. the grain bed is sparged after the first runnings are drained.

A drain without a duration from a vessel with a drain rate (see Vessel) takes the time to drain the volume it moves, from the brew res of q (brewed with models when not given). Vessels are not checked for being free: see overlaps.

source

See also makespan, overlaps and vesselchart.

Targets and solving

BrewQuiver.solve! — Function
solve!(q::Quiver, targets...; vary, models = (;), rtol = 1e-8, maxiter = 50) -> Solution

Changes the parameters vary (an AbstractParameter or a vector of them, as many as the targets) of q so that its brew meets the targets (each a Target, or node => f => goal), by Newton's method with a Jacobian by finite differences, damped to keep amounts positive and fractions between 0 and 1. E.g. the grain bill for an original gravity and the sparge water for a pre-boil volume:

solve!(q, :fermentor => gravity => 1.052u"sg", :kettle => volume => 28u"L";
       vary = [Amount(:pale, :crystal), Amount(:sparge_water)])

q is left with the solution, also when the solver does not converge (see the field converged of the Solution).

BrewQuiver.attribution — Function
attribution(res::BrewResult, q::Quiver, product) -> Dict{Symbol, Float64}

The share of each node of q in the product (a node): the fraction of its contents that goes into making the product, following the liquor along the arrows. What a node leaves behind (spent grain, trub, yeast cake, the beer left in a tank) is allocated to the products its other arrows serve. For a lot (a node without predecessors), the share is the fraction of the lot used for the product: what remains on the shelf is not allocated.

The shares of a lot in all the products of q add up to the fraction of the lot used, unless some of it only reaches nodes whose contents are all left behind.

BrewQuiver.recipe — Function
recipe(res::BrewResult, q::Quiver, product; name = string(product), author = "",
       notes = nothing) -> BeerJSON.RecipeType

The BeerJSON recipe of a product (a node) of the brew res of q. BeerJSON recipes are linear, so the recipe holds the shares of the lots used for the product (see attribution): its ingredient additions, by their stage and timing, and the water of its mash steps and sparges, gathered over the mash tuns that serve it, with the procedures of the mash tun, kettle and fermentor that serve it most. Its batch size is the volume reaching the fermentors for the product, its pre-boil size the volume in the kettles, its efficiency the brewhouse efficiency of those fermentors, and its gravities, alcohol, color and carbonation those of the product, before any priming.

writejson(path, lib::Library)

Writes the ingredients of lib to a BeerJSON document at path, as fermentables, hop varieties and cultures, with their stock as inventory. Counts of cultures (packs, vials) are written under "inventory_count", an extension, since BeerJSON's inventory of cultures holds masses and volumes; other BeerJSON readers ignore it.

source
writejson(path_or_io, q::Quiver; results = nothing)

Writes the quiver q as a JSON Graph Format (JGF) document: its nodes (with their initial contents, vessel, and the free data of nodeview), its arrows as edges, in order, its equipment, and its ingredients as BeerJSON catalogue records. With the brew results of q, each node also gets the values computed by it (volume, gravities, alcohol, bitterness, color, carbonation, temperature, and the mash pH of mashes), for other readers of the file; readjson ignores them, and they are computed again by brew.

writejson("bitter.quiver.json", q; results = brew(q))
q = readjson(Quiver, "bitter.quiver.json")

BrewQuiver.Library — Type
Library()
Library(sources...)

A library of ingredients (Fermentable, Hop, Culture) by name, with their stock: a mass, a volume or a count, or nothing when not known. The sources are BeerJSON documents (BeerJSONParser) or the paths of BeerJSON files, whose fermentable, hop and culture records (with their inventory) are loaded in order.
