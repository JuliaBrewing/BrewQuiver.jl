A BeerJSON recipe

BeerJSON is the JSON format for brewing recipes, read and written in Julia by BeerJSON.jl. A BeerJSON recipe builds its quiver: its ingredients as lots, the water, and the stages of the mash tun, the kettle, the fermentor and the packaging.

Reading a recipe

We take the RR Summer Bitter from the samples of BeerJSON.jl:

using Unitful, UnitfulBrewing, BrewingFormulas, BeerJSON, BrewQuiver

file = joinpath(pkgdir(BeerJSON), "test", "samples", "converted", "RRSummerBitter.json")
recipe = only(readjson(BeerJSONParser, file).beerjson.recipes)
recipe.name
"RR Summer Bitter"

Its quiver

q = Quiver(recipe; calibration = BrewhouseEfficiency())

Each ingredient became a lot, with an arrow per addition, timed as in the recipe; the mash steps became mash rests (and infusions, decoctions), the boil a Boil, the fermentation steps Ferments. The ingredient records convert to the working types with Fermentable(addition), Hop(addition) and Culture(addition).

The volumes a recipe does not give are solved for its own targets: the sparge water for its pre-boil volume, the evaporation rate and the kettle loss for its batch size:

volume(q[:sparge_water_1].initial)
29.358896083444318 L

An equipment (a BeerJSON EquipmentType, given as Quiver(recipe; equipment = ...) or taken from the document) gives the grain absorption, the dead spaces, the losses and the boil rate, as Vessels of the quiver.

Calibration

A recipe states the brewhouse efficiency of the brewery it was written for. With BrewhouseEfficiency, the conversion of the mash is adjusted so that the quiver reproduces it; with NoCalibration (the default), the efficiency follows from the processes of the quiver:

res = brew(q)
(brewhouse_efficiency(res, q), quantity(recipe.efficiency.brewhouse))
(0.7499999995992039, 75.0 %)
q0 = Quiver(recipe)
brewhouse_efficiency(brew(q0), q0)
0.8834669890267864

The beer

beer = res[:beer]
(og = original_gravity(beer), fg = gravity(beer), abv = abv(beer), ibu = ibu(beer), color = srm(beer))
(og = 1.0450489739696107 sg, fg = 1.0109539531301333 sg, abv = 4.521811823604397 %, ibu = 36.503927217093114 IBU, color = 6.749726486346211 SRM)

When a recipe states its own estimates (original_gravity, ibu_estimate, color_estimate), they may differ from these: the recipe's software may use other formulas for the hop utilization or the color, and its losses are not always stated. Choose the models with brew(q; models = ...).

What the importer reads

  • Fermentable, hop, culture and miscellaneous additions, with their timing (mash, boil, first wort, whirlpool, dry hop, priming).
  • The water additions, as the WaterProfile of the water, and the "water agent" additions (gypsum, calcium chloride, lactic acid, ...) as Salts and Acids; the distilled water pH of fermentable records.
  • The mash steps: infusions (the water that reaches their temperature, when they give no amount), temperature steps and decoctions (their boil and return).
  • The boil steps, a whirlpool as a hop stand, the chilling.
  • The fermentation steps, and priming as a priming sugar.

Where a recipe is incomplete (a culture without attenuation, a dry hop without time), the importer warns and takes a default. A Library can complete it: Quiver(recipe; library = lib) (see Ingredient library).

And back

A quiver exports as BeerJSON recipes, one per product (see Provenance and export). A recipe exported from a quiver and imported back, with the equipment of its kettle, brews to the same wort and beer.
