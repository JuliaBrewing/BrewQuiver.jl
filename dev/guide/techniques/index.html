Mashes, decoctions, stands and dry hops

All the techniques below are built from the same few arrows; no special process is needed.

Step mashes

A step mash is a chain of MashRests in the mash tun, with infusions of water before them. The first rest converts the grain; the next ones bring the mash to their temperature (the temperature of a node is the temperature of the mixture that reached it).

q = Quiver()
add!(q, :pilsner, pale => 5u"kg")
add!(q, :dough_in, water(12u"L"; temperature = 57u"°C"))
add!(q, :infusion, water(6u"L"; temperature = 98u"°C"))
add!(q, :mash, :protein_rest, :sacch, :mashout; vessel = :mashtun)
connect!(q, [:dough_in, :pilsner] => :mash)
connect!(q, :mash => :protein_rest, MashRest(temperature = 52u"°C", time = 15u"minute"))
connect!(q, [:protein_rest, :infusion] => :sacch)
connect!(q, :sacch => :mashout, MashRest(temperature = 66u"°C", time = 60u"minute"))
res = brew(q)
(res[:sacch].liquor.temperature, res[:mashout].liquor.temperature, gravity(res[:mashout]))
(64.60238904867947 °C, 66.0 °C, 1.0693045780395036 sg)

The infusion brings the mash to the temperature of the mixture; the rest then holds it at its own temperature, heating as needed.

Decoctions

A decoction takes a part of the mash (a Transfer of a fraction) to the kettle, boils it, and returns it before the next rest, which it brings to temperature by mixing:

q = Quiver()
add!(q, :pilsner, pale => 5u"kg")
add!(q, :strike, water(15u"L"; temperature = 55u"°C"))
add!(q, :mash, :rested, :remaining, :sacch, :done; vessel = :mashtun)
add!(q, :decoction, :boiled; vessel = :kettle)
connect!(q, [:strike, :pilsner] => :mash)
connect!(q, :mash => :rested, MashRest(temperature = 52u"°C", time = 20u"minute"))
connect!(q, :rested => :decoction, Transfer(35u"percent"))   # the thick part
connect!(q, :rested => :remaining)
connect!(q, :decoction => :boiled, Boil(time = 15u"minute"))
connect!(q, [:remaining, :boiled] => :sacch)
connect!(q, :sacch => :done, MashRest(temperature = 66u"°C", time = 45u"minute"))
brew(q)[:sacch].liquor.temperature
67.05704257819455 °C

The boil of a decoction evaporates little (10%/hr, BrewQuiver.DECOCTION_EVAPORATION, when the vessel gives no rate).

Hop stands

A hop stand (whirlpool) follows the boil, as part of the Boil:

Boil(time = 60u"minute", stand = 20u"minute", stand_temperature = 80u"°C")

The hops keep isomerizing during the stand at the rate of its temperature relative to boiling (isomerization_factor of BrewingFormulas). A hop added At(70u"minute") stands for the last 10 minutes; one added with remaining = 0u"minute" (at flameout) for all of the stand.

q = Quiver()
add!(q, :wort_in, wort(25u"L", 1.050u"sg"; temperature = 100u"°C"))
add!(q, :saaz, saaz => 60u"g")
add!(q, :kettle, :hopped; vessel = :kettle)
connect!(q, :wort_in => :kettle)
connect!(q, :saaz => :kettle, Transfer(30u"g"; remaining = 60u"minute"))
connect!(q, :saaz => :kettle, Transfer(30u"g"; remaining = 0u"minute"))      # flameout
connect!(q, :kettle => :hopped, Boil(time = 60u"minute", stand = 20u"minute", stand_temperature = 80u"°C"))
ibu(brew(q)[:hopped])
13.965042297562553 IBU

First wort hops and additions by time

Timings are relative to the transformation that follows the node the ingredient arrives at: Remaining(t) with t left of it, At(t) after it starts. An ingredient arriving without timing is in for the whole transformation (first wort hops, the grain in the mash).

Dry hops

Dry hops are additions to the fermentor with a timing (Remaining(3u"d") or At(2u"d")); they add no bitterness, and keep the beer they absorb when racked, with hop_absorption on the drain or the vessel:

q = Quiver()
add!(q, :wort_in, wort(20u"L", 1.060u"sg"; temperature = 19u"°C"))
add!(q, :citra, citra => 100u"g")
add!(q, :yeast, Culture("US-05"; attenuation = 78u"percent") => 1)
add!(q, :fermentor, :beer; vessel = :fermentor)
add!(q, :keg)
connect!(q, [:wort_in, :yeast] => :fermentor)
connect!(q, :citra => :fermentor, Transfer(; remaining = 4u"d"))
connect!(q, :fermentor => :beer, Ferment(temperature = 19u"°C", time = 14u"d"))
connect!(q, :beer => :keg, Drain(hop_absorption = 5u"L/kg"))
res = brew(q)
(ibu(res[:keg]), volume(res[:keg]), volume(leftover(res, :beer)))
(0.0 IBU, 19.387733032760575 L, 0.5000000000000004 L)

Fly sparging and double mashes

  • Fly sparging is a sparge with drains in between, or a single drain of the grain bed that receives the sparge water: the quiver does not model the gradient, only the balance of what goes in and out.
  • A double mash drains the wort of a mash into another mash, as its strike liquor.

Carbonation

Bottle conditioning is a Prime (the priming sugar in the node is fermented, with its CO₂ dissolved); forced carbonation a Carbonate to a level in volumes. priming_mass gives the sugar for a carbonation.
