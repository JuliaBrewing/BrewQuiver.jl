Extending BrewQuiver

A new transformation

A transformation is a subtype of AbstractTransformation that implements transform(t, contents, models) -> (contents, released): the contents after it, and what it released from the quiver, as a NamedTuple (water, co2) of masses (negative for what it took in). With a time field, its duration is that time; otherwise it implements duration.

A cold crash, which only cools the beer:

struct ColdCrash <: BrewQuiver.AbstractTransformation
    temperature::typeof(1.0u"°C")
    time::typeof(1.0u"d")
end
ColdCrash(; temperature = 2u"°C", time = 2u"d") = ColdCrash(temperature, time)

BrewQuiver.transform(t::ColdCrash, c::Contents, models) =
    (with_temperature(c, t.temperature), (water = 0.0u"kg", co2 = 0.0u"kg"))

q = single_batch()
add!(q, :crashed; vessel = :fermentor)
disconnect!(q, :beer => :bottling)
connect!(q, :beer => :crashed, ColdCrash())
connect!(q, :crashed => :bottling, Drain())
liquor(brew(q)[:crashed]).temperature
2.0 °C

The transformation should conserve mass: what it changes in the contents is either released (water, co2) or moved within them (extract to alcohol, ...). The tests of BrewQuiver check the balance of the whole quiver with leftovers and released; do the same for a new arrow.

The formulas a transformation needs belong in BrewingFormulas.jl, with their references.

Saving it

The files of quivers write the arrows they know. A new type is registered with BrewQuiver.register_json_type: its values are then written as objects of their fields, and rebuilt with its constructor of all the fields, in order.

BrewQuiver.register_json_type(ColdCrash)
path = writejson(tempname() * ".json", q)
readjson(Quiver, path)[:crashed => :bottling]
1-element Vector{AbstractArrow}:
 Drain()

Models

The models of a brew are those of BrewingFormulas (brew(q; models = (bitterness = Rager(),))). A new model is a new subtype there, with its methods; the quiver uses it as soon as it is passed to brew.

Timings and solids

New timings (AbstractTiming) and solids (AbstractSolid) are possible, but touch the internals of the arrows (how they mix, split and dissolve); open an issue first.
