Concepts

Why a quiver

A recipe is a list: ingredients, a mash schedule, a boil schedule, a fermentation. It describes one wort that becomes one beer. Many brews do not fit a list: a parti-gyle takes several worts of decreasing strength from the same mash; a double mash runs the wort of one mash through another; a brewery blends the beers of several fermentors into products of its own. In all of them, streams split and merge.

BrewQuiver describes a brew as a quiver, the mathematicians' name for a directed multigraph: a set of nodes, and arrows between them, with possibly several arrows joining the same two nodes. Here the graph is acyclic, since brewing goes forward in time.

  • A node is the contents of a vessel at a stage of the brew: the pale malt on the shelf, the strike water, the mash before and after its rest, the wort in the kettle before and after the boil, the beer in the fermentor.
  • An arrow is a process taking contents from a node to another: a transfer, a drain, or a transformation (a mash rest, a boil, a fermentation, ...).

Contents: conserved quantities

The state of a node is a Contents: a Liquor and a list of solids. Both hold conserved quantities only:

Liquor fieldWhat it isUnit
watermass of waterkg
extractmass of dissolved extract (sugars, dextrins)kg
alcoholmass of ethanolkg
isoalphamass of iso-alpha acidsmg
colorcolor unitslb·°L
co2mass of dissolved CO₂g
temperaturetemperature°C
mineralsdissolved ions (Minerals)mg

Solids are ingredients as added (Portion, with an amount and a timing), spent grain (Residue), hop matter (HopMatter) and yeast (YeastCake).

Because every field is an amount, mixing is addition (a + b, with the temperature of the mixture) and splitting is scaling (0.3 * c), and both are exact. Everything a brewer usually reads off a wort, its volume at 20 °C, gravity, original gravity, ABV, IBU, color, carbonation, is derived from these amounts with BrewingFormulas:

using Unitful, UnitfulBrewing, BrewingFormulas, BrewQuiver

w = wort(20u"L", 1.050u"sg")
(volume(w), gravity(w), w.extract)
(20.0 L, 1.05 sg, 2.5967265319537507 kg)
half = 0.5 * w
(volume(half), gravity(half))
(10.0 L, 1.05 sg)
diluted = w + water(5u"L")
gravity(diluted)
1.04005443074054 sg

Nodes

Nodes are labelled by symbols and added with add!:

  • lots have initial contents: an ingredient and an amount (pale => 4.5u"kg", us05 => 1 for a count), or a liquor such as water(16u"L"; temperature = 75u"°C");
  • stages start empty and are filled by arrows: add!(q, :mash, :rested; vessel = :mashtun). The vessel tags the stages that live in the same vessel, for its equipment and the timeline.

Whatever arrives at a node is mixed, so blending needs no special process.

Arrows

Arrows are added with connect! and come in three kinds:

  • Transfer moves a uniform part of the contents: a volume, a mass, a fraction, or what is left (Transfer(), the default). The ingredients it brings may have a timing: Transfer(25u"g"; remaining = 10u"minute") for a hop with 10 minutes of boil left.
  • Drain moves the liquid only, leaving behind the solids, the liquid the grain absorbs and the liquid the vessel retains.
  • Transformations (MashRest, Boil, Chill, Ferment, Prime, Carbonate) turn all the contents of a node into the next stage of the vessel. They act on what is in the node: the grain mashed in, each hop for its own time, the culture pitched, the priming sugar. A transformation is the only arrow leaving its node.

Several arrows may join the same nodes, e.g. two additions from the same lot of hops. Arrows that would create a cycle are refused.

Brewing

brew computes all the nodes in topological order and returns a BrewResult: res[label] is the contents of a node, and res[source => destination] what its arrows did (ArrowResults). What no arrow takes from a node is its leftover, and what the arrows release from the quiver (evaporated water, CO₂) is released. Over the whole quiver,

\[\sum \text{initial contents} = \sum \text{leftovers} + \text{released},\]

which the tests check for every brew. The products are the nodes nothing leaves.

After a change of a node or an arrow, rebrew! recomputes only what is downstream of it.

Models

The derived quantities depend on models of BrewingFormulas: the conversion between gravity and extract, hop utilization, color, ABV. The defaults are DEFAULT_MODELS; a brew can choose others:

res = brew(q; models = (bitterness = Rager(), color = Daniels()))

The results then compute with the same models (res.models).

Calibration

Real brewhouses do not convert all the potential extract. A MashRest has a conversion (0.95 by default), and the losses of the quiver (absorption, dead spaces, trub) follow from its arrows. A BeerJSON recipe can be calibrated to its stated brewhouse efficiency with BrewhouseEfficiency; see A BeerJSON recipe.
